Beginner Guide So You Want to Start Warhammer 40K — The Honest Beginner's Guide
By Toby  ·  May 25, 2026  ·  8 min read

Let me be straight with you from the start.

Warhammer 40,000 is one of the most rewarding hobbies I've ever encountered. It's also one of the most intimidating to walk into cold. Thousands of miniatures. Dozens of factions. Multiple game systems. From the outside, it looks like a wall of complexity that only lifts for people who already know the secret handshake.

Here's what nobody tells you: every single person who plays this game started exactly where you are right now. Knowing nothing. Slightly overwhelmed. Wondering if it's worth it.

It is. And the path in is much simpler than it looks.

The hobby has three parts — you don't have to love all three

Building — assembling plastic miniatures. Satisfying in the same way a detailed model kit is. Painting — from quick tabletop-standard paint jobs to museum-quality display pieces. You choose how far you want to go. Playing — the actual game. Missions, objectives, dice rolls.

Some players buy pre-painted miniatures and only care about the game. Some build and paint obsessively and barely play. Most are somewhere in between. The hobby meets you where you are.

Before you buy anything — come play first

This is the most important advice in this article. Don't spend a dollar until you've played a game.

At Just for Fun Gaming, play time is free. We have demo models, we have tables, and we have people who know the game and are happy to walk a complete beginner through their first battle. It takes about an hour and costs you nothing except an evening.

Pick your first army

Rule one: pick the army whose models you think look the coolest. Not the most powerful in the current meta. The one that makes you think "I want to paint that."

Space Marines are the classic entry point. Death Guard are rotting and grotesque and somehow beautiful. Tyranids are a swarm of alien biological horrors. Tau win by shooting from maximum distance. Orks are chaotic, loud, and deeply fun. Necrons refuse to stay dead.

What to buy first — the Combat Patrol box

Games Workshop makes Combat Patrol boxes specifically for new players. Each contains a curated selection for one faction — enough to play small introductory games at a significant discount. Most run $110–130. Buy one. Build it. Come in and play.

Your first game

Your first game will feel confusing. This is normal. There are a lot of rules and you won't remember all of them. By your third game, you'll feel like you know what you're doing. By your tenth, you'll have opinions about list-building. It happens faster than you'd expect.

Come into Just for Fun Gaming any Tuesday–Friday from 4:30 PM, or Saturday–Sunday from 3 PM. Tell us you want to learn 40K. We'll take it from there — for free.

Community Why Local Game Stores Matter More Than Ever
By Toby  ·  May 21, 2026  ·  4 min read

Amazon will get you a board game in two days. Sometimes one. The price is usually fine. The convenience is undeniable.

What Amazon cannot do is introduce you to the three other people in your area who play the same obscure miniature wargame you just discovered. It cannot look at your half-painted army and tell you exactly which contrast paint will fix the problem you're having. It cannot host a Tuesday night campaign that becomes the highlight of your week. And it definitely cannot stay open until 10 PM so you can finish the final round of a game you've been playing for three hours.

The third place

Sociologists talk about "third places" — spaces that aren't home and aren't work, where community actually forms. Game stores, at their best, are one of the few genuinely welcoming third places left for people who don't drink.

That's what we're trying to build here. A place where you can show up not knowing anyone, sit down at a table, and leave with people you're going to see again next week.

Why we started Just for Fun Gaming

I've been playing tabletop games my whole life. I kept thinking: Danbury deserves a place like this. So after a lot of planning, I stopped waiting for someone else to open it.

Play time is free because I want the barrier to entry to be zero. Come in. See if you like it. The community is the point — the snacks and game sales are just how we keep the lights on.

Tabletop RPG D&D vs Pathfinder: Which Is Right for Your Group?
By Toby  ·  May 20, 2026  ·  6 min read

Both Dungeons & Dragons (5th Edition) and Pathfinder (2nd Edition) are excellent tabletop RPGs. They share the same DNA — both descend from the original D&D of the 1970s — but they've evolved into genuinely different games.

D&D 5th Edition: accessibility first

Fifth Edition was designed to bring in new players. Character creation is fast, the core rules fit in your head within a few sessions, and there's an enormous amount of free material available. Best for: new players, groups that want to focus on story and roleplay, and DMs who want creative flexibility.

Pathfinder 2nd Edition: depth and precision

Pathfinder 2e went in the opposite direction. The rules are comprehensive and precise — there's a right answer for most situations. Character building is deep with a feat-based system that lets you construct exactly the character you envision. Best for: players who enjoy optimizing characters and want tactical combat with clear rules.

The honest answer

If you've never played a tabletop RPG: start with D&D. If you're an experienced player who burned out on 5e feeling shallow: Pathfinder 2e will absolutely reignite your enthusiasm.

We run intro sessions for both at Just for Fun Gaming. Come play a session for free and let the game sell itself. We also have a regular Thursday night Pathfinder campaign with room for new players — sign up for the newsletter to get notified when spots open.
